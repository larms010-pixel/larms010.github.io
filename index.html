Public Policy

As a second-year public policy student, I am very familiar with the importance of having systems that justly serve the people who are subject to them. Additionally, as someone who is pre-law and wants to become a lawyer, I am very interested in learning more about our state’s criminal justice system, as well as how to reduce the lifelong consequences that often come with interacting with the criminal justice system. I am excited about the prospective opportunity to deepen my understanding of practices that can help people smoothly transition into civilian life after interacting with our state’s criminal justice system. My interest in criminal justice and law originated in high school, after participating in the YMCA Youth and Government program, but has expanded much further since. After learning more about the mission of organizations like the Innocence Project, I began to view becoming a public defender more strongly as a career path. The research that I have done and knowledge that I have gained through my studies have furthered my interest in how to reduce the lifelong consequences that often come with interacting with the criminal justice system. With recidivism rates in California decreasing in recent years, it is important to ensure that rates continue to drop so that people who have already served time can stop being punished after already having been administered justice. A research study that interested me was “Militarization Fails to Enhance Police Safety or Reduce Crime but May Harm Police Reputation”, by Jonathon Mummolo. I would explain this study to a nonacademic audience by explaining that his study focuses on SWAT deployment throughout the US in communities of color, and how effective it actually is. He found that SWAT is often deployed in communities of color, in situations that they shouldn’t have been. SWAT being called in often has the opposite effect of what it was intended for, not lowering crime but instead causing a more negative view of police, which increases crime in those communities. While participating in pre-law organizations like Phi Alpha Delta, I learned more about criminal law and the issues within our system, and I believe that the skills I’ve gained will allow me to perform well as a research intern. While participating in the Youth and Government program, I presented research findings to program participants with little other known information about the subjects. Additionally, I have experience in writing policy recommendations while serving on the Green Campus Action Plan’s policy team. Within my roles in the Legal Minds society, I gained experience in writing newsletters, designing digital flyers and posts, and creating fundraiser advertisements, later on improving my note-taking and event planning skills as secretary. Meanwhile, working for campus dining has dramatically increased my teamwork and communication skills. If able to participate in the program, I hope to learn more about California’s criminal justice system while developing skills that will support my future career goals. I believe that serving in this role would help strengthen my research and critical thinking abilities, while expanding my communication skills.
